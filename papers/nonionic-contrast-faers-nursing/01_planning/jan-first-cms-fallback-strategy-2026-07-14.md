# JAN-first / CMS-fallback submission strategy

Date: 2026-07-14
Paper: `nonionic-contrast-faers-nursing`
Approval log: `APR-20260714-143914-2d163b`

## Approved route

The current author-approved route is:

1. First submit a Journal of Advanced Nursing (JAN)-facing version.
2. If JAN rejects or desk-rejects the manuscript, retarget to Current Medical Science (CMS).

This supersedes the previous CMS-first recommendation.

## Why JAN is being added

JAN gives the strongest nursing identity among the current serious SCI/SSCI nursing options. If the author wants the paper to read as a nursing contribution rather than a general pharmacovigilance study, JAN is a coherent first-shot target.

The reason to try JAN first is strategic rather than probability-based: it may preserve the nursing identity better if accepted. It is not the lowest-difficulty route.

## Main risk

The present study is based on FAERS disproportionality and time-to-onset analysis. It does not include:

- nurse participants;
- nursing workflow variables;
- a nursing intervention;
- direct patient/nursing outcome evaluation;
- prospective or local safety-process data.

Therefore, JAN may consider the manuscript insufficiently nursing-led if the nursing contribution appears only in the Discussion. The JAN version must make the nursing surveillance problem visible from the title, abstract, introduction, and interpretation of results.

## Required JAN-facing framing

One-sentence argument for the JAN version:

> In FAERS, disproportionality and time-to-onset analyses of non-ionic iodinated contrast media identify reporting signals and early clustering patterns that can inform radiology nursing surveillance priorities, while remaining hypothesis-generating and not proving incidence, causality, or intervention effectiveness.

Required changes before JAN submission:

- Title: foreground radiology nursing surveillance, not only FAERS pharmacovigilance.
- Abstract: make the nursing surveillance question explicit in Background/Aim.
- Introduction: define the nursing problem as risk recognition, observation timing, escalation preparedness, and patient education around contrast administration.
- Results: keep pharmacovigilance results intact; add reader-oriented signposting showing which findings are relevant to nursing surveillance.
- Discussion: convert nursing content into a bounded surveillance-priority framework; do not claim that nursing interventions reduce adverse-event rates.
- Cover letter: acknowledge that the study is secondary pharmacovigilance and argue its relevance to nursing risk prioritisation.

## CMS fallback logic

If JAN rejects the paper for scope or nursing-contribution reasons, the CMS fallback should remove the heavy nursing-first framing and restore the manuscript as a general medical pharmacovigilance study with clinical monitoring implications.

CMS fallback changes:

- Title: return to non-ionic iodinated contrast media safety signals and TTO patterns.
- Abstract: make FAERS pharmacovigilance the primary purpose.
- Introduction: emphasise contrast-media safety, spontaneous-reporting evidence, and clinical monitoring.
- Discussion: keep nursing surveillance as one clinical application rather than the core disciplinary claim.
- Cover letter: differentiate clearly from the similar contrast-media spontaneous-reporting article already published in CMS.
