# Nursing Open manuscript revision plan

Paper ID: `nonionic-contrast-faers-nursing`  
Target route: Nursing Open first; Current Medical Science fallback  
Date: 2026-07-14  
Status: proposed; awaiting author approval

## 1. Core positioning

### Current safest one-sentence argument

In spontaneous FAERS reports involving four non-ionic iodinated contrast media, this study uses disproportionality and time-to-onset analyses to identify reportable adverse-event patterns and early clustering that may help radiology nursing teams prioritize surveillance and escalation, while not estimating incidence, proving causality, comparing absolute drug safety, or testing nursing interventions.

### Chinese working version

本研究基于 FAERS 自发报告，采用不成比例分析和发生时间分析，识别四种非离子型碘造影剂相关不良事件报告信号及早期聚集模式，为放射护理团队设定观察重点和升级处理窗口提供假设生成依据；但不估计真实发生率、不证明因果关系、不比较药物绝对安全性，也不证明护理干预有效。

## 2. Target article type and journal constraints

Recommended Nursing Open article type: **Empirical Research Quantitative**.

Journal requirements that affect rewriting:

- Maximum 8,000 words.
- Up to 10 figures/tables.
- Structured abstract, maximum 300 words.
- Double-blind peer review: anonymized main document plus separate title page.
- Free Format submission is allowed at first submission.
- Research article needs a relevant EQUATOR checklist.
- References should be recent, English, primary-source-oriented, and generally within the journal's reference expectations.
- Required statements likely include data availability, funding, conflict of interest, ethics approval, and patient/public contribution.

## 3. Main strategic change

The manuscript should stop reading like a generic pharmacovigilance paper with nursing suggestions added at the end. It should read as:

> A nursing-facing pharmacovigilance study that translates FAERS signal and time-window patterns into radiology-nursing surveillance priorities.

This means nursing relevance must appear in:

1. Title.
2. Abstract Aim / Implications / Impact.
3. Introduction gap.
4. Discussion interpretation.
5. Conclusion.

It should not be forced into Methods or Results beyond neutral, evidence-grounded wording.

## 5. Section-by-section revision plan

### 5.1 Results first

Reason: project writing logic requires evidence-first rewriting.

Actions:

- Verify the reported count: 14,780 eligible reports.
- Verify the 24 positive PT signals and ensure all algorithms actually reported are described consistently.
- Recheck the denominator for TTO: 12,588 evaluable reports.
- Verify the 81.56% within 24 hours figure.
- Preserve result wording as observation, not interpretation.
- Use `signal of disproportionate reporting`, not incidence or causal risk.
- Create or revise tables so each main claim has one clear evidence anchor.

Output expected:

- Clean Results outline.
- Claim-evidence table update.
- List of `[VERIFY]` numbers that require author/data confirmation.

### 5.2 Methods second

Reason: Nursing Open reviewers will look for reproducibility and trustworthy secondary-data handling.

Actions:

- Specify FAERS data period, quarters, download source, and deduplication method.
- Define the four non-ionic ICM search terms and mapping rules.
- Define AE coding level: MedDRA preferred term (PT) and system organ class (SOC).
- State disproportionality algorithms actually used and thresholds.
- Define TTO calculation and exclusion rules for impossible/missing dates.
- Explain software and version if known.
- Add ethics statement: public de-identified spontaneous-report data; formal ethics review likely not required, but author institution wording should be confirmed.
- Add reporting guideline candidate: likely STROBE or RECORD/STROBE-style checklist for observational secondary-data reporting, to be finalized before submission.

Output expected:

- Reproducible Methods draft.
- Missing-methods checklist for the author.

### 5.3 Introduction third

Reason: Introduction must be rebuilt around actual results and Nursing Open readers.

Paragraph jobs:

1. Field stake: non-ionic ICM are widely used; AEs require prompt recognition and escalation in imaging settings.
2. Nursing problem: radiology nursing teams need practical surveillance priorities, not only drug-level safety summaries.
3. Knowledge gap: large spontaneous-report signal patterns have not been clearly translated into nursing-sensitive observation windows and event priorities.
4. Present study: FAERS disproportionality and TTO analyses of four non-ionic ICM to generate bounded surveillance priorities.

Actions:

- Avoid overclaiming novelty with words like `first`, `unique`, or `comprehensive`.
- Keep the nursing problem visible from paragraph 1 or 2.
- Do not present nursing interventions as already proven by this study.

### 5.4 Discussion fourth

Reason: Discussion is the highest desk-reject risk area for Nursing Open because the dataset is not a nursing intervention dataset.

Paragraph jobs:

1. Central finding: signals and early clustering suggest where nursing surveillance should focus.
2. Extravasation and immediate reaction implications: connect to observation, vascular access monitoring, early escalation, and documentation.
3. Renal/CA-AKI and delayed-event boundary: discuss carefully without causal claims.
4. Relationship to guidelines and prior work: only cite verified ACR/ESUR/FDA/FAERS/pharmacovigilance sources.
5. Nursing practice implication: frame as prioritization and vigilance, not intervention efficacy.
6. Limitations: FAERS reporting bias, missing denominators, duplicate/missing data, confounding by indication/procedure, inability to infer incidence/causality, lack of nurse-level variables.
7. Future research: prospective nursing workflow or quality-improvement validation.

Actions:

- Delete or downgrade unsupported claims that a standardized nursing pathway reduces events.
- Keep any specific nursing action only if supported by guideline or external evidence.
- Mark all unverified practice suggestions as `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`.

### 5.5 Title and abstract near the end

Reason: Nursing Open requires a structured abstract; it should reflect stable Results and Discussion.

Possible title direction:

- `Adverse-event reporting patterns of non-ionic iodinated contrast media and implications for radiology nursing surveillance: Empirical Research Quantitative`

This is a working title, not final. It may need shortening to satisfy Nursing Open's title rule.

Structured abstract plan:

- Aim(s): To identify FAERS reporting signals and time-to-onset patterns for four non-ionic ICM and discuss implications for radiology nursing surveillance.
- Design: Retrospective pharmacovigilance study using spontaneous reporting data.
- Data Sources: FAERS, exact period to be verified.
- Methods / Review Methods: disproportionality and TTO analyses.
- Results: only verified headline numbers; no p values/CIs in abstract.
- Conclusion: signals and early clustering may inform surveillance priorities.
- Implications: radiology nursing observation and escalation planning.
- Impact: what problem, main finding, and target user.
- Reporting Method: final checklist to be inserted.
- Patient/Public Contribution: likely `No Patient or Public Contribution`.
