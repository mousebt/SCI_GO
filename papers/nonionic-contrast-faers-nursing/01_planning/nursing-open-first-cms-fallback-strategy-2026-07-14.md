## Why Nursing Open is now first

Nursing Open preserves the nursing-journal identity while being more explicit as an open-access nursing outlet already in the current journal library. It carries a mandatory APC, but the recorded USD 3,560 cost is within the author's acceptable range. It is the selected first target after the author rejected the JAN route on cost grounds.

This is a nursing-identity route, not the lowest-risk route. CMS remains the lower-modification and better method-fit fallback.

## Main risk

The present study is based on FAERS disproportionality and time-to-onset analysis. It does not include:

- nurse participants;
- nursing workflow variables;
- a nursing intervention;
- direct patient/nursing outcome evaluation;
- prospective or local safety-process data.

Therefore, Nursing Open may still judge the work as pharmacovigilance rather than nursing research if the nursing contribution is limited to the Discussion.

## Required Nursing Open-facing framing

One-sentence argument for the Nursing Open version:

> In FAERS, disproportionality and time-to-onset analyses of non-ionic iodinated contrast media identify reporting signals and early clustering patterns that can inform radiology nursing surveillance priorities, while remaining hypothesis-generating and not proving incidence, causality, or nursing intervention effectiveness.

Required changes before Nursing Open submission:

- Title: foreground radiology nursing surveillance and contrast-media safety.
- Abstract: make the nursing surveillance question explicit in Background/Aim.
- Introduction: define the gap as translating spontaneous-reporting signals into nursing surveillance priorities.
- Results: keep pharmacovigilance results intact; add reader-oriented signposting for nursing relevance.
- Discussion: present a bounded surveillance-priority framework and delete unsupported intervention-effect claims.
- Cover letter: explicitly state that the study is secondary pharmacovigilance and that its nursing contribution is risk-prioritisation, not tested intervention effectiveness.

## CMS fallback logic

If Nursing Open rejects the paper for scope or insufficient nursing contribution, the CMS fallback should reduce the nursing-first framing and restore the manuscript as a general medical pharmacovigilance study with clinical monitoring implications.

CMS fallback changes:

- Title: return to non-ionic iodinated contrast media safety signals and TTO patterns.
- Abstract: make FAERS pharmacovigilance the primary purpose.
- Introduction: emphasise contrast-media safety, spontaneous-reporting evidence, and clinical monitoring.
- Discussion: keep nursing surveillance as one clinical application rather than the core disciplinary claim.
- Cover letter: differentiate clearly from the similar contrast-media spontaneous-reporting article already published in CMS.
